================================================================================
NOVA SCHEDULING — WHAT TO PUT IN THE SHEET
A guide for managers filling out the input workbook
================================================================================

WHAT THIS IS
------------
You give the system your people, your tasks, how good each person is at each task,
when they can work, and when you're busy. The system builds a weekly schedule that
puts your most efficient people where they matter most — especially during rushes —
so service is fast and wait times drop. It never schedules overtime on its own and it
flags anything that touches workers' rights for you to decide.

Two ways to give us your data:

  1. THE WORKBOOK (recommended).  Fill in schedule_input_template.xlsx (the tabs below).
     It is read directly and exactly as you typed it. Nothing is sent to any outside
     service, so employee names and pay stay in-house. This is the privacy-safe path.

  2. PLAIN-TEXT NOTES (optional).  If you'd rather paste messy notes ("Alex does nights,
     Sam is fast on the fryer…"), an AI assistant reads them and turns them into the same
     structured data, which you then review and correct before anything is scheduled.
     NOTE: this path sends your notes (including names and pay) to a third-party AI
     provider to interpret them. Use the workbook if that data must not leave your systems.

This guide describes the WORKBOOK. Only yellow cells are yours to edit; each tab has one
grey example row showing the format — type over it or delete it.


THE EFFICIENCY METRIC (the heart of it)
---------------------------------------
For every person, for every task they can do, you give an EFFICIENCY RATING.

  • HIGHER = MORE EFFICIENT.  A 5 is faster/better at that task than a 3.
  • It is per TASK, not per person.  Someone can be a 5 on the fryer and a 2 on the grill.
  • Where does the number come from?  Whatever you measure. The clearest basis is TIME:
    how long, on average, it takes that person to do that task (e.g. seconds per order,
    tickets per hour). Faster → higher rating. You convert your measurement into the
    rating; a simple way is:
        5 = fastest / your best at this task
        4 = above average
        3 = solid, meets expectations
        2 = slow, still learning
        1 = much slower than others
  • Only rate tasks a person actually does. A rating is also what makes them ELIGIBLE for
    that task — no rating means the optimizer won't assign them to it.

The optimizer adds these ratings up across the schedule and favors the arrangement with
the highest total — and it counts busy windows more (see RushWindows > priority), so your
strongest people land in the rushes.


FRONT OF HOUSE vs BACK OF HOUSE
-------------------------------
People stay on their side of the house. A front-of-house person (server, cashier, host)
is never put on a back-of-house task (cook, grill, fryer, dishwasher), and vice versa.
This is automatic and based on each task's role (set on the Tasks tab):

  BACK of house : Cook, Dishwasher   (and prep/line)
  FRONT of house: Server, Cashier, Host   (and bar/runner)
  EITHER        : Manager

So just rate people on the tasks they really do, and the boundary takes care of itself.


THE TABS — WHAT TO FILL IN
================================================================================

ROLES  (reference list; extend if needed)
  The broad job categories (Cook, Server, Cashier, Host, Dishwasher, Manager). These set
  the front/back-of-house side for every task. You rarely need to change this.
  Columns: role | description

TASKS  (the stations the schedule staffs — the pay/skill unit)
  Each specific station a person can be assigned to, e.g. Fry Station, Grill, Expo,
  Register, Floor, Host Stand, Dish Pit.
  Columns:
    task                 — the station name (e.g. "Fry Station")
    role                 — which broad Role it belongs to (sets front/back of house)
    description          — free text
    default_hourly_rate  — the usual wage for this station (used only to respect pay/OT
                           rules; pay is NOT what the efficiency schedule optimizes for)
    target_units_per_hour— optional, informational

EMPLOYEES  (one row per person)
  Columns:
    employee_id          — a stable id you invent (E001, E002, …); must match other tabs
    name                 — display name
    primary_role         — their main Role (also sets their front/back-of-house side)
    secondary_roles      — other Roles they can cover, comma-separated (optional)
    employment_type      — Full-time / Part-time (optional)
    min_hours_week       — the minimum weekly hours you want to give them (optional; if the
                           schedule can't reach it, you get a FLAG — it's never forced)
    max_hours_week       — hard weekly cap. The schedule NEVER exceeds this; unmet demand
                           becomes a gap for you rather than silent overtime. (required)
    max_shifts_week      — optional hard cap on number of shifts
    overtime_threshold_hours — reference only (e.g. 40). Recorded so you can see who's near
                           overtime. It does NOT authorize the system to schedule overtime.

AVAILABILITY  (one row per time block a person can work)
  Columns:
    employee_id          — must match an Employees row
    day                  — Mon Tue Wed Thu Fri Sat Sun
    available_start      — 24-hour HH:MM (e.g. 09:00)
    available_end        — 24-hour HH:MM (e.g. 17:00)
    preference           — Preferred / Available / Avoid (optional)
  A person can work a rush window only if one of their blocks fully covers it.

PROFICIENCY  (THIS IS WHERE THE EFFICIENCY RATINGS GO)
  One row per person per task they can do.
  Columns:
    employee_id          — must match an Employees row
    task                 — must match a Tasks row (and be on their side of the house)
    proficiency          — THE EFFICIENCY RATING, 1–5, higher = more efficient (see above)
    hourly_rate          — that person's wage for this task (optional; blank = task default)
    throughput_per_hour  — optional raw metric (e.g. units/hour); informational for now
  A person with no Proficiency row for a task is simply never assigned that task.

RUSHWINDOWS  (your demand — and when you're busy)
  One row per (window, task): how many of a task you need in a given window.
  Columns:
    day                  — Mon Tue Wed Thu Fri Sat Sun
    window_label         — a name for the window (e.g. "Lunch", "Dinner Rush")
    window_start         — 24-hour HH:MM
    window_end           — 24-hour HH:MM
    task                 — which station this demand is for (must be on the Tasks tab)
    required_staff       — how many people you need at that task in that window
    priority             — HOW BUSY this window is: 1 = normal, 2 = busy, 3 = slammed.
                           Higher priority tells the optimizer to put your most efficient
                           people here first, to keep wait times down. Blank = 1.

POLICY  (your operating decisions)
  Key/value settings. The important one:
    overtime_allowed     — Yes/No. Your call. The system never schedules past anyone's
                           max_hours; if a rush can't be covered within normal hours you
                           get a gap to decide on. Overtime is never automatic.


FORMATS & RULES (quick reference)
---------------------------------
  • Times: 24-hour HH:MM (09:00, 17:30).
  • Days: the three-letter codes Mon Tue Wed Thu Fri Sat Sun.
  • employee_id must be identical everywhere it appears.
  • Every task you use must exist on the Tasks tab; every role on the Roles tab.
  • Rate a task only for people who actually do it (rating = eligibility).


WHAT YOU GET BACK
-----------------
A weekly schedule that:
  • covers every window it can, placing your most efficient people — weighted toward your
    busiest windows — to cut wait times;
  • keeps everyone on their side of the house;
  • never exceeds anyone's hour/shift caps (no silent overtime);
  • FLAGS, for your decision: any window that could only be covered with overtime, and
    anyone left under their minimum hours.
The system makes no recommendations about layoffs or pay — it only schedules the people
and ratings you give it.
